# Editorial contract

## Core promise

Make a knowledge Short that would still be worth watching if the final Linko sequence disappeared. Linko is evidence of how the creator preserves and connects ideas, not the subject of the argument.

## Non-negotiable tests

1. The viewer can repeat one defensible idea after watching.
2. One Short carries one thesis; adjacent ideas become later episodes.
3. The source, verified context, and creator inference remain distinguishable.
4. Linko appears once, late, and performs a meaningful research action.
5. Removing the final product footage does not break the intellectual conclusion.

## Angle selection

Prefer a question with genuine tension over a broad topic label. A strong angle has one surprising but supportable claim, enough context to stand alone, a concrete example, a point of view beyond summary, a visible source moment, and room for a qualification.

Propose two or three angles and rank them for originality, evidence strength, completeness within the target duration, and visual potential. Do not combine multiple strong angles merely because they came from the same source.

## Conversational language

Default to a first-person discovery frame when truthful: `I watched... and one question stayed with me.` Continue in the same voice throughout the Short.

Prefer short clauses, concrete nouns, and ordinary verbs. Keep specialist vocabulary only when it is the lesson and explain it immediately. Avoid academic throat-clearing, stacked abstractions, SaaS copy, and feature narration.

For a 45–60 second Short, begin around 110–140 total spoken words and adjust after a timed voice take. A natural pause is more valuable than maximum word density.

## Linko placement

Use Linko during approximately the final 15–20% to add the source, reveal the saved resource, open a relevant note, and show one meaningful connection. Do not list features in VO. Let the action imply capture, connection, or continuation while the knowledge narration continues.

Use a real screen recording for final release. A screenshot prototype is only a disclosed draft and remains a release blocker.

## Visual language

- Open with real motion, not a video thumbnail or static trailer frame.
- Let the primary source carry evidence; use trailers and cinematic B-roll as texture.
- Remove player chrome when allowed and preserve attribution in the asset manifest.
- Use deliberate crops and focus moves instead of constant fake camera motion.
- Keep word-for-word captions separate from editorial headlines.
- Inspect collision with burned-in subtitles and mobile platform controls.

## Voice direction

Describe a performance, not a celebrity. Specify regional accent, age range, relationship to the listener, warmth, curiosity, pace, energy, pronunciation, and negative directions.

Do not clone or closely imitate an identifiable person's voice without authorization. State when a review voice is generated.

## Outro truthfulness

Use a generic line such as `Follow for the next idea.` when no public Linko destination exists. Add `Full notes in Linko` or `Follow my notes on Linko` only after verifying that viewers can access the promised destination and action.

## Common failure patterns

- **Product-demo genre drift:** the Short explains Linko more than the idea.
- **Context hiding:** a provocative line depends on information never given to the viewer.
- **Inference laundering:** the creator's thesis is presented as the source's quotation.
- **Static-motion substitution:** screenshots are animated and described as recordings.
- **Overpacked essay:** several angles compete within one minute.
- **Audio reset:** source audio feels pasted between unrelated narration takes.
- **False CTA:** the ending promises a public note or follow action that does not exist.
